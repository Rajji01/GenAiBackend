Problem: no oversell in a flash sale

The through-line

The whole platform exists to answer one hard question: when 50,000 people hit "book" on the same show at once, how do you never double-sell a seat, never crash, never lose money, and always give a clean answer? Week 1 solves oversell on one machine. Week 2 splits the booking decision into its own service and coordinates with a saga. Week 3 adds real money (payment) and reliable events (outbox → notification). Week 4 lands it all on managed AWS. Same problem, growing answer.

WEEK 1inventory-service — solve oversell first

new: inventory-service. The whole flash-sale correctness problem, solved on one machine with a two-tier lock. → SEAT_LOCK

WEEK 2+ booking-service — the saga orchestrator

new: booking-service. It owns the booking decision and coordinates inventory over REST; a failed step compensates instead of locking. Idempotency-Key makes a retried request one booking. → SAGA_LAB

WEEK 3+ payment + notification + the outbox

new: payment-service (Adapter/Factory/Strategy, 2-step auth+capture) and notification-service (idempotent consumer). The outbox makes "state change + event" atomic; a recovery sweep heals a saga that crashed mid-flight. → PAYMENT_LAB

WEEK 4+ AWS landing — same services, managed infra

new: not a service — a home. The same four services run as ECS Fargate tasks behind one ALB, on RDS Multi-AZ, creds in Secrets Manager, isolated by a VPC + SG chain, all as Terraform. Design + skeleton done; real AWS is Rajat's. → CLOUD_LAB

Where it's heading

The roadmap continues past Week 4: async decoupling (SQS/SNS/DLQ), a read-heavy catalog with caching + CDN, a flash-sale virtual waiting room, DynamoDB for the idempotency store, security hardening (Cognito/WAF/KMS), observability (X-Ray tracing across the whole saga), and CI/CD. Each still answers the same one question — correct booking under load — at a larger scale. See ROADMAP.md.
